For businesses that already work, held back by the admin around them.

This isn't for a startup with an idea. It's for an operating business with customers, staff, and a growing pile of repetitive work nobody wants to own.

  • You already have customers and steady revenue
  • Someone on the team spends hours a week on repetitive admin
  • Leads go missing because follow-up depends on remembering
  • You're running several bits of software that don't talk to each other
  • Data gets typed into one system after being typed into another
  • There's nobody internally whose job is to fix any of this

Four phases, in order.

Each one stands on its own. You can stop after the assessment and take the plan elsewhere. I'd rather that than build something you're not sure about.

1

Opportunity assessment

I sit with how the business actually runs, not how the org chart says it does. Where the repetitive work is, where revenue leaks out, and which of it AI or automation could genuinely take over. Some things on that list will be “don't bother”, and I'll say so.

2

The plan

What should be automated, what should use AI and what shouldn't, which systems need to connect, what gets built first, what it costs and what it should give back. One document you could hand to someone else.

3

Implementation

The workflows get built and your systems get connected. AI goes in where it earns its place. Everything is tested on real cases before it touches a customer.

4

Ongoing improvement

Systems drift and things break quietly. Ongoing means I watch what's running, fix what stops, improve what's clunky, and look for the next thing worth doing.

Fair things to ask.

Is this just ChatGPT with extra steps?

Some of it is a language model doing a job it's good at. Plenty of it is plumbing between systems with no AI in it at all. If a simple automation beats an AI one, you get the simple one.

Are you going to tell me to replace my staff?

No. Almost always the win is taking the tedious half of a job away so the person can do the half that actually needs them.

What if the assessment says do nothing?

Then it says that, and you've spent a small amount to avoid spending a large one.

What does it cost?

The assessment is a fixed one-off, scoped to the size of the business. Builds are quoted once we know what we're building, and support is month to month if you want it.

Do I have to get you to build it afterwards?

No. The assessment stands on its own. If you'd rather take the plan to someone else, or build it in-house, that's completely fine.

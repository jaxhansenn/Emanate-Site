When it's worth building

When the workaround has become the job.

Most businesses run on a patchwork: a spreadsheet, an inbox, three apps that don't talk to each other, and one person who remembers how it all fits together. That works until it doesn't.

  • The same details get typed into two or three systems
  • Your process lives in one person's head
  • You pay for software you only use a corner of
  • Clients send documents by email and someone files them by hand
  • Reports get built by copying numbers out of other tools
  • You've looked for an app that does it, and none quite fit

How a build goes

Small first, then bigger once it's proven.

STEP 01

Map the process

Before anything is built, I sit with how the work actually moves through the business: who touches what, where it stalls, and what's being done twice.

STEP 02

Scope and price it

What it does, what it connects to, how long it takes and what it costs. Agreed in writing before a line is built, so there's no open-ended bill.

STEP 03

Build in stages

You see working pieces early, not a big reveal at the end. It's much easier to change direction while it's still cheap to.

STEP 04

Launch and support

Tested on real cases, then switched on, with a plain summary of what does what. If something breaks, you text me and I fix it.

Questions

Fair things to ask.

Why not just use off-the-shelf software?

Often you should, and I'll tell you so. Custom makes sense when you've tried the obvious tools and they force your team into workarounds, or when the real problem is getting several tools to work together.

What does it cost?

It depends entirely on what it does. Every build is scoped and priced before it starts, so you know the number before you commit. You'll get a real figure once I've seen the process.

Can I start small?

Yes, and I'd usually recommend it. Build the one piece that hurts most, see it work, then decide on the next.

What happens if it breaks?

You text me and I fix it. Ongoing support is monthly if you want it.

Where does our data go?

We agree on that before anything is built: what's stored where, what AI is allowed to see, and what never leaves your systems. If your industry has rules about it, they shape the design from the start rather than getting patched in later.

Got a process that doesn't fit the software?

Fifteen minutes to walk me through it. I'll tell you whether it's worth building, and what it would take.
